# Problems encountered and fixes (symptom -> cause -> fix)

These are things actually encountered while rigging a bird model (items 1-11) and a cat model (items 12-19). If the same thing happens, start here.

1. **In the pecking pose, the torso surface at the shoulder pokes out over the folded wing** -> heat weights give the shoulder-side torso influence from the neck bone, but the shell is attached only to `wing_fold` (a child of the body) -> lock the torso weights in the region the shell covers (`wing_fold_cover`, `dilate` 0.4 cm) to 100% `body`, and blend with a smoothstep into the original values over the outer 1 cm.
2. **The deformation is rough or the subdivision result is odd** -> the armature modifier comes after the subdivision -> put the armature first (`move_armature_first`). The glb export applies subdivision and interpolates the weights.
3. **Torso vertices are dragged by the legs, wings, or root** -> heat weights use all deform bones -> just before the heat step, temporarily turn off `use_deform` on every bone except the ones to use (including root). Restore it afterwards.
4. **The glb contains IK controls or check actions** -> the default export includes all bones and actions -> use `export_def_bones=True` (deform bones only) and `export_animation_mode='NLA_TRACKS'`, and put only the actions to export on muted NLA tracks. The IK result is sampled and baked onto the deform bones.
5. **Part of a part that was hidden by scaling it small remains** -> that part's vertices share weights with other bones too -> attach a part to be hidden 100% only to the hiding bone (or its children).
6. **The check poses look blended together** -> the keys are interpolated with Bezier -> `rig_test` inserts keys with CONSTANT interpolation. In Blender 5 there is no `action.fcurves`, so find the F-curves through layer -> strip -> channelbag (`action_fcurves`).
7. **When the .blend is opened, the rig stands in the flight pose** -> the glTF export leaves the last exported animation on the pose, and it is saved in that state (an earlier bird .blend was in this state) -> reset the pose after exporting and then save. The render script also resets the pose directly and draws `rest`.
8. **Re-importing the glb creates an `Icosphere`** -> the glTF import creates the shape used to display bones -> it is harmless. Hide it in renders.
9. **When Blender is run through the MCP, a saved function cannot be run** -> the MCP safe mode blocks file reads, exec, numpy, and computed setattr -> run rigging in the background with `blender -b --factory-startup -P`. Use the MCP only for visual checks that a person looks at.
10. **Rendering with only the action changed on the same frame gives results that seem suspect** -> in fact it is evaluated correctly. If it looks wrong, first print the pose bone values to check (in the bird model, the cause of the `rest` image coming out in the flight pose was item 7).
11. **When the engine crossfades two poses, the hidden wing flies in from tens of cm away** -> if a parent bone's scale is 0, the glTF export (which recomputes local transforms from world matrices) cannot define the child bone's translation, rotation, and scale, and writes arbitrary values (a bird model's `pose_ground`: `wing_fore.L` translation (-0.23, -0.04, 1.31) m, 0.375 m from the shoulder mid-blend) -> make the hiding scale `hideScale` (0.001), not 0. Then the children keep their bind values, and during the blend the wrist moves only from 0 to 12 cm from the shoulder. The cost: floating-point error grows by a factor of 1/hideScale, so the child translation differs from the bind value by at most 2e-5 m (0.02 mm). With 0.01 it is 1e-6 m, but the hidden part remains at a 3 mm size. `inspect_rig.py compare` shows this difference from the old file not as a FAIL but separately as `INFO … hide scale changed` and `children of zero-scaled bones … rest-local` lines.
12. **Using `upper_arm.*` in a weight rule for a center part stops with a `TypeError`** -> `.*` was only replaced with the part's side, and `body` has no side -> for a center part, expand `.*` to both sides (`expand(names, side)`). The bird template has no `.*` in its center parts, so the result is the same.
13. **In a check pose, rotating the upper arm or thigh does not move the leg** -> the two-bone IK overwrites the chain's rotation (the target is at the bind position) -> to see it as FK like the engine, put `"ik": false` in `testPoses` to key 0 on the influence of the IK and copy-rotation constraints. The influence is restored to 1 on save, so the IK is still usable in the .blend. `pose_stand` is baked with IK on and differs from the bind by at most 0.04 degrees.
14. **When the front leg is extended far forward (76 degrees), the area behind the shoulder folds into a V, and when crouching, the hollow behind the knee overlaps (39 pairs)** -> the upper arm and thigh skin is a rounded area on the side of the torso, so weights change sharply at its edge -> Laplacian smoothing only near the joints (`smooth`: shoulder r 9 cm, hip joint r 8 cm, elbow and knee r 3.5 cm). The hollow-of-the-knee intersections went to 0 and the shoulder crease became shallower. Doubling the radius and iterations does not reduce the contact (item 15).
15. **In the gallop pose, hundreds of self-intersection pairs appear** -> most are lines where skin touches skin (a thigh folded forward more than 50 degrees against the belly, a forearm folded back against the underside of the chest, a shin folded more than 75 degrees against the back of the thigh). Linear skinning has no collision, so weights cannot remove them -> with `render_poses.py --intersections`, see which bone it is, tell from the red-face images whether it is a face that broke through or a line of contact, then measure the clean angle range with `sweep_limits.py` and write it in the README.
16. **In the engine, putting X+ to lay the ears back tips them forward** -> the local Z of a bone with roll 0 differs by bone direction (down for a bone facing forward, back for a leg pointing down, forward for an ear pointing up) -> do not guess; measure with `rig_axes.py` on both the .blend and the glb and decide the sign per meaning. In the cat, only `earBack` differed from the guess (+1) (it was -1).
17. **A bone that points almost -Y (forward) may not have local X sideways even at roll 0** -> Blender decides the axes by the minimal rotation from +Y to the bone direction, and when the bone points forward and also tilts sideways, that rotation axis becomes oblique and local X stands up (when the sideways tilt is similar to the downward tilt) -> in the `localX` column of `rig_axes.py`, check that it is +X (near 1.00). The cat's `hind_paw` has a small sideways tilt, so it is 0.98. A bone that points exactly -Y (`root` in both templates) uses the axes Blender rotated 180 degrees about Z, so local X is -X.
18. **In the engine, folding the front leg "lower segment backward" makes the elbow look like a knee pushed forward, and in the gallop gather pose the forearm digs into the chest** -> the cat's front-leg elbow points backward, so the forearm folds forward and the wrist (front paw) folds backward. Folding the forearm backward is hyperextension -> this is not a rig problem. When fixing game motion, tell them to fold the front leg with `forearm` X- (forward) and `front_paw` X+ (backward).
19. **The rigged .blend is twice the size of the input** -> `save_blend` also includes the source images of the projection materials (`<id>_project_*`) left by plz-sheet-to-model -> this is intended (it opens as a single file). An earlier bird .blend (15.8 MB) is the same. To make it smaller, delete the projection materials at the model stage.
